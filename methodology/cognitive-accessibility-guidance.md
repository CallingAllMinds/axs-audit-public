# Cognitive Accessibility Guidance

AXS Audit includes checks informed by W3C WAI cognitive accessibility guidance, including Making Content Usable for People with Cognitive and Learning Disabilities.

This work sits alongside WCAG-informed technical accessibility checks. It is intended to help organisations recognise barriers that may not always be captured by automated conformance testing alone.

## Why cognitive accessibility matters

People may experience digital barriers related to:

- Attention
- Memory
- Language processing
- Reading
- Executive function
- Time pressure
- Sensory load
- Decision-making
- Task sequencing
- Anxiety or cognitive fatigue

These barriers can affect neurodivergent users, people with cognitive and learning disabilities, people experiencing stress or fatigue and many others.

## Areas considered by AXS Audit

AXS Audit may consider:

- Clarity of language
- Length and density of content
- Predictability of navigation
- Visibility of key actions
- Complexity of forms
- Error prevention and recovery
- Consistency of labels and interface patterns
- Readability and scannability
- Cognitive load within journeys
- Availability of support or explanation
- Use of icons, images and visual cues
- Whether users can pause, review or recover

## Practical outcomes

The purpose of cognitive accessibility review is to help teams create digital experiences that are easier to understand, navigate and complete.

Recommendations may include:

- Simplifying content
- Improving headings
- Reducing unnecessary choices
- Making instructions clearer
- Improving error messages
- Breaking complex journeys into clearer steps
- Making help easier to find
- Reducing visual or interaction overload
- Supporting alternative ways to understand content

## Important note

Cognitive accessibility is not a replacement for WCAG. It extends the practical lens of accessibility by considering how users understand, process and act on information.
