# AXS Audit Reporting Structure

AXS Audit reports are intended to help organisations understand accessibility barriers and take practical action.

## Typical report sections

A report may include:

1. Executive summary
2. Accessibility score or summary outcome
3. Priority findings
4. WCAG-informed issues
5. Cognitive accessibility observations
6. Usability and journey friction
7. Page or journey-level findings
8. Recommended remediation
9. Suggested prioritisation
10. Next steps

## Finding structure

Each finding may include:

- Title
- Description
- User impact
- Affected page, flow or component
- Relevant WCAG criterion or guidance
- Severity
- Priority
- Evidence
- Recommended action
- Suggested owner

## Severity

Severity may consider:

- Whether users are blocked
- Number of users likely affected
- Importance of the affected journey
- Frequency of the issue
- Legal or compliance risk
- Ease of remediation
- Impact on disabled or neurodivergent users

## Remediation guidance

Recommendations should be written in clear, practical language. They should help product, design, content, compliance and development teams understand what to change and why.

## Limitations

An automated report should not be treated as the full accessibility picture. It should be used alongside human review, user testing, assistive technology testing and ongoing monitoring.
